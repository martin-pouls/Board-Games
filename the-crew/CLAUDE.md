# The Crew

**The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine** (Kosmos), cooperative trick-taking for 2–5 players with all 50 missions; with 2 players the dummy JARVIS joins. Common rules: root [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md). Rules: [docs/rules.md](docs/rules.md) (rules summary, mission table §14, App decisions). [docs/implementation-plan.md](docs/implementation-plan.md) explains the design.

**Two editions, one codebase.** The same code is also deployed as **The Crew: Mission Deep Sea** at `/deep-sea/` ([../deep-sea/](../deep-sea/CLAUDE.md) holds only its deploy files). The edition is fixed per deployment by `EDITION` (`planetNine` | `deepSea`): at build time for the frontend (`__EDITION__`, `src/lib/edition.ts` for terms, `theme-deep-sea.css`; `npm run dev:deep-sea` on :5179), at runtime for the server (default port :8085, `data/deep-sea.db`; a game of the other edition is not found). Deep Sea rules, mission table and the 96 task cards: [docs/deep-sea/rules.md](docs/deep-sea/rules.md); design and work packages: [docs/deep-sea/implementation-plan.md](docs/deep-sea/implementation-plan.md). A change here deploys both apps. Keep edition switches in few places (`missionTable`, `dealTaskCards`, `nextItem`, `edition.ts`), and the UI terms in `edition.ts` (rocket/submarine, JARVIS/Tonoja, commander/captain).

## Session, intents and per-player view

- **Session model:** a game (lobby code) is one sitting of a crew. The host picks a mission in the lobby; the crew plays attempts (`playing → debrief`) until it is won, then goes on to the next mission or back to the lobby. The logbook (attempts, distress signal, completed) lives in the game. `Game.Options` (so far `Distress`, off by default) are set by the host in the lobby (and may come with `POST /api/games`: the host's last options, remembered per device in `the-crew:lobby-options`, Deep Sea: `deep-sea:…`); a snapshot without them loads with everything off.
- **Retention:** `GAME_RETENTION=720h` (30 days, for the logbook; the privacy policy says so). Extra REST route: `GET /api/missions`.
- **Intents:** set options, select mission, start mission, adjust/skip tokens (23, 40), answer poll, pick seats (commander), pick task (draft), send/skip distress, choose pass card, hand over a task (5 players), communicate, play card, give up, next attempt, next mission, back to lobby, end game. The commander acts for JARVIS and makes every "decide together" call.
- **Per-player view:** own hand and playable cards (the dummy's on its turn, for the commander), every member's card count, tricks won and communication, JARVIS's face-up cards and whether a hidden card lies under each, the tasks (face-down ones without their card), the objectives with their status, poll answers, the viewer's own pass choice and own side of the last pass or draw, the current and last trick, the actions allowed now, and in the debrief the result and all hands. Never other humans' hands, JARVIS's hidden cards, other players' pass choices or the deck. Events are public and card-free for private moves.

## Domain modelling

- **Missions are data:** `missions_planet9.go` mirrors rules.md §14 row by row (`MissionDef`). A test compares it against an independently written table.
- **Tasks and special objectives are one concept:** an `Objective` (kind + owner + parameters), evaluated as a **pure function of the completed tricks** (`Objective.Status(History)`), never stored. Fail early, fulfil only once it can no longer fail; every open objective fails after the last trick. Token order (`orderViolation`) uses the trick in which each task was fulfilled (rules §7).
- **The trick rules are edition-agnostic** (`trick.go`) and know nothing about seats, the dummy or missions. Only `Member.Playable` and `actorFor` know about the dummy.
- **One `Attempt` per deal;** a new attempt replaces it. Members are seats (with 2 players seat 2 is the dummy), referenced by seat index on the wire.
- **The setup is derived:** only `Step` and the open `Poll` are stored; `nextItem` derives the pending setup item from the `MissionDef` and the attempt. `Actors()` is derived per step and never empty while playing.
- **The RNG is injected** (`Shuffle`, `IntN`). `sim_test.go` (and `sim_deepsea_test.go`) play random legal actions through every mission × 2–5 players and check card conservation, actors and the hiding rules after every action.
- **Editions:** `Game.Edition`, the mission table per edition (`missions_planet9.go`, `missions_deepsea.go`), the task cards (`tasks_deepsea.go`, `taskcard.go`), the open `ObjectiveKind` set (Deep Sea kinds in `objective_deepsea.go`), the Deep Sea setup in `setup_deepsea.go`. No Planet Nine names in `trick.go`, `objective.go` or `seat.go`: the domain says trump, dummy, commander; the UI says rocket, JARVIS, commander.

## Look and feel

Planet Nine, "rulebook page": warm rulebook paper with navy ink and a royal-blue accent in light mode, midnight navy with a blue glow at the top and a sky-blue accent in dark mode. Oxanium for the display (title, headings, card values), Sofia Sans Semi Condensed for the UI. Cards are drawn by us: white, the value and the colour's line symbol once in the top-left corner (colour-blind support), and Planet Nine's night print in a window beside them (the neon symbol on the colour's dark glow; rockets: a white rocket among stars, dark value). Small sizes (tasks, the communicated card) have no window.

The table fits a phone without scrolling (optimised for 2 players with JARVIS), top to bottom: header row (code, mission, rules, give-up flag), crew strip (a row per member: name, counts, tasks, communicated card; JARVIS with its 7 stacks, hidden cards as a dark edge underneath), the mission line (objectives and communication rules, only when there are any), trick area (the last trick stays until the next trick's first card; the open tasks lie here during the setup, five a row, scrolling past ten), then the bottom block: your own row, one 44 px button row and the hand. Each task lies with its owner on a square task token (order mark, then ✓/✗; navy in light mode, lilac in dark mode). A communicated card sits at the right end of its owner's row, a little larger, with a green ring and the round radio token (top: highest, middle: only, bottom: lowest, "?" when hidden). The button row (`ActionRow`) says what happens in play: a clear Play button once a card is picked, an outlined hint on your turn ("Pick a blue card"), plain text while others play, and Communicate (icon and label) at every trick start while you may still communicate, whoever leads. Communicating: tap Communicate, the hand veils the cards that can't be shown, tap one, the button states it ("Show 2 as lowest"), ✕ cancels. Every other decision (draft, polls, picks, pass, distress, handover) stays in the decision panel above the button row. In the hand only the picked card lifts; cards that can't be picked get a veil in the table colour (not transparency). Nothing is sticky. Card motion throughout. The debrief is an inline panel in the table (JARVIS's stacks give way to everyone's hands), not the usual game-over sheet: it comes after every attempt and leads straight into the next one.

Mission Deep Sea shares this layout but keeps its own look until its theme round: its tokens, Nunito and Space Grotesk (`--app-font-*` in theme-deep-sea.css), its card face and glyphs (the `edition` switches in `PlayingCard` and `SuitGlyph`), and its task cards (`TaskCard` tells them apart by the task, not the edition). The dev fixtures `stressView2` and `dsStress2/3/5` hold the fullest tables; `presets` in the fixture files start a fixture with a card picked or in the communicate flow.
